Appeals Timeliness Is Leaving MA Star Ratings. It Is Not Leaving MA Oversight.

CMS is shrinking the Medicare Advantage Star Ratings, and the two Part C appeals measures are among the first to go. That does not make prior authorization and appeals timeliness less important. The same data still decides program audit findings, Independent Review Entity outcomes, the prior authorization metrics every MA plan now posts, and, increasingly, the evidence in Star Ratings litigation.

What changed in 2026

In April 2026, CMS finalized the Contract Year 2027 Medicare Advantage and Part D rule (CMS-4212-F, 91 FR 17384, published April 6, 2026). Its Star Ratings section removes a group of mostly administrative and process measures. The measures listed under “Removing Measures” in the rule are:

  • Plan Makes Timely Decisions about Appeals (Part C) and Reviewing Appeals Decisions (Part C)
  • Special Needs Plan (SNP) Care Management (Part C)
  • Call Center – Foreign Language Interpreter and TTY Availability (Part C and D)
  • Complaints about the Health Plan / Drug Plan (Part C and D)
  • Medicare Plan Finder Price Accuracy (Part D)
  • Statin Therapy for Patients with Cardiovascular Disease (Part C)
  • Members Choosing to Leave the Plan (Part C and D)
  • Customer Service and Rating of Health Care Quality (Part C)

CMS had also proposed removing Diabetes Care – Eye Exam, but kept it. According to the rule, the removals apply to the 2027 measurement period and the 2029 Star Ratings, except the call center and statin therapy measures, which come out beginning with the 2028 Star Ratings. The rule also adds a Part C Depression Screening and Follow-Up measure, declines to implement the Health Equity Index reward, and keeps the historical reward factor.

In practice, appeals performance in 2025 and 2026 still feeds the 2027 and 2028 Star Ratings. Plans that treat the appeals measures as already gone are early by two rating years.

The two appeals measures and how they worked

Both Part C appeals measures were built from Independent Review Entity (IRE) data rather than from plan self-reporting, which is part of why they carried weight with CMS and with plans:

MeasureWhat it capturedUpstream operational data
Plan Makes Timely Decisions about AppealsHow often the plan decided reconsiderations within the required timeframe, including cases it should have forwarded to the IRE on timeRequest received timestamps, decision clocks, extensions, IRE auto-forward dates
Reviewing Appeals DecisionsHow often the IRE agreed with the plan's decision to uphold a denialDenial reasons, clinical criteria applied, case files sent to the IRE

Neither measure was really about appeals in isolation. Each one tested whether the plan's original organization determination, often a prior authorization decision, was timely, well documented and defensible. That underlying obligation does not change when the measure leaves the Star Ratings.

The CY 2028 proposal: 15 measures

On November 20, 2026, CMS issued a second proposed rule for contract year 2028, CMS-4214-P2 (the first, CMS-4214-P, was issued October 16, 2026). According to the CMS fact sheet, it would:

  • Reduce the Star Ratings to a set of 15 measures, focused on outcomes, with “a small number of process measures in areas of high clinical importance where contracts' current performance is low.”
  • Apply the change to the 2028 measurement period and the 2030 Star Ratings.
  • Remove the reward factor, and limit the hold harmless for the improvement measures to contracts at 4.5 stars or higher.
  • Change the cut point methodology, in part to account for the proposed ADVaNCE MA model, which would test a separate “Diamond Ratings” system in select states.
  • Require MA contracts with in-network providers to include a term requiring those providers to submit prior authorization requests and documentation through the plan's FHIR-based Prior Authorization API, with annual testing.

CMS describes the goal as raising the bar for high Star Ratings and the Quality Bonus Payments tied to them. The fact sheet does not list which measures would be removed; that detail is in the proposed rule itself (FR Doc 2026-23960), along with the comment deadline.

The pattern. Process measures are leaving the Star Ratings, but CMS is pushing the same processes into contract terms and technical requirements. The prior authorization proposal is a clear example: a provider submission channel that used to be optional becomes a contract obligation.

Where timeliness data still counts

Removing a Star Ratings measure removes one consequence of slow or weak decisions. It leaves the requirement, and several other places where CMS looks at the same data.

1. Decision timeframes still apply

Under CMS-0057-F, MA organizations must decide standard prior authorization requests within 7 calendar days and expedited requests within 72 hours, and must give a specific reason for denials. These requirements took effect January 1, 2026 (see what is already in effect). Reconsideration timeframes in 42 CFR Part 422, Subpart M, are unchanged, and adverse reconsiderations still go to the IRE automatically.

2. Program audits still test organization determinations, appeals and grievances

CMS program audits include an Organization Determinations, Appeals and Grievances (ODAG) review. Plans submit universes of their cases, and CMS samples them for timeliness, notice content and clinical appropriateness. Findings can lead to corrective action plans and civil money penalties. None of that depends on whether a measure is in the Star Ratings.

3. Public prior authorization metrics

CMS-0057-F also requires impacted payers, including MA organizations, to post prior authorization metrics on their websites each year, starting March 31, 2026: approval and denial rates, approvals after appeal, extensions, and average and median decision times. These numbers are public, comparable across plans, and available to regulators, providers, brokers and plaintiffs alike. See the fact sheet and compliance timeline.

4. IRE overturn rates

The IRE still reviews every adverse reconsideration. An IRE overturn is a data point about the original prior authorization decision whether or not CMS publishes it as a star measure.

5. The Prior Authorization API

The FHIR Prior Authorization API is due January 1, 2027 for MA organizations. If the CY 2028 proposal is finalized, in-network providers would be contractually required to use it. A request that arrives through the API carries a machine-readable timestamp and status, which makes the decision clock easier to reconstruct for anyone reviewing it. The CRD, DTR and PAS guide explains how those requests flow.

Why fewer measures raise the stakes

MA plans have challenged their Star Ratings in court repeatedly since 2023, and several large insurers sued over the 2027 ratings released in October 2026. Many of these cases turn on how CMS treated a single measure or cut point, because one measure can move a contract across the 4-star line that determines Quality Bonus Payments. Health Affairs has written about this “Stars docket.”

A 15-measure set makes each remaining measure heavier, so the value of an accurate record goes up. Plans that challenge a rating, or answer an audit, need to show their own data: when a request arrived, when the clock started, what was decided and why. That record is built from the same prior authorization and appeals operations the appeals measures used to sample.

What a plan's data should be able to show

Whatever the final measure set looks like, an MA plan's utilization management and appeals data should be able to answer these questions without a manual reconciliation project:

  • For every prior authorization and appeal, when the request was received, by which channel, and when the decision clock started.
  • Which requests were expedited, which were extended, and why.
  • The decision, the specific denial reason, and the criteria applied.
  • Which adverse reconsiderations were forwarded to the IRE, on what date, and what the IRE decided.
  • Annual approval, denial, appeal-approval and decision-time figures that match what the plan posted publicly.
  • An ODAG universe that can be produced from the system of record, not rebuilt from spreadsheets.

The practical advice is to keep these metrics independent of any particular measure definition. Measures come and go between rule cycles. Clean, timestamped decision data lets a plan answer whichever question CMS, an auditor or a court asks next.

FAQ

Are the Part C appeals measures being removed from Star Ratings?

Yes. The CY 2027 final rule removes Plan Makes Timely Decisions about Appeals and Reviewing Appeals Decisions for the 2027 measurement period and the 2029 Star Ratings. They still count in the 2027 and 2028 Star Ratings.

What does the CY 2028 proposed rule do to Star Ratings?

It proposes a 15-measure set for the 2030 Star Ratings, removes the reward factor, narrows the improvement hold harmless and changes the cut point method. It is a proposal and may change.

Does prior authorization timeliness still matter for MA plans?

Yes. The 7-day and 72-hour timeframes still apply, program audits still test them, the IRE still reviews adverse reconsiderations, and the metrics are posted publicly each year.

Does CMS-0057-F apply to Medicare Advantage?

Yes. MA organizations are impacted payers under CMS-0057-F, with a January 1, 2027 date for the FHIR APIs. See the full FAQ.
